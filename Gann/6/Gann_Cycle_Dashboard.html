What is the Natural Cycle?

W.D. Gann's Natural Cycle identifies calendar dates within each month that historically produce significant market Highs (H) or Lows (L). By studying decades of price data, these dates reveal a recurring "natural" rhythm — markets tend to reverse near the same calendar dates year after year. Traders use this matrix to anticipate potential turning points without needing price data.

  • H = Historical High on that date in that month-year
  • L = Historical Low on that date in that month-year
  • H/L = Both events occurred (high then low, or vice versa) within the same month

What is the Dynamic Cycle?

The Dynamic Cycle counts actual trading days (excluding weekends and Indian market holidays) between consecutive market Highs and Lows. Gann discovered that markets repeat turning points at harmonic intervals: 45, 90, 120, 135, 180, 270, 360 trading days. When the current date falls at one of these harmonic counts from a previous major high or low, the market has a higher probability of reversing.

  • The Start Date is a significant historical high or low
  • The Current/End Date is when the cycle count lands
  • The high/low of the candle on the Start Date acts as major support/resistance

W.D. Gann – Father of Cycle Theory

William Delbert Gann (1878–1955) was an American finance trader who developed unique technical analysis tools. He believed that markets move in predictable geometric, mathematical, and time-based cycles derived from natural law.

Natural Cycle Theory

Markets follow calendar-based natural time cycles. Key dates within each month recur as significant highs or lows across decades. By mapping these dates in a Year × Month grid, traders can see which specific day-numbers are "natural" turning points for a given instrument.

  • No price data required — purely time-based
  • Works across years (20–30 year look-back)
  • Per-instrument specificity (NIFTY cycles differ from BANKNIFTY)
  • Identifies "natural" days: 7th, 14th, 21st, 28th common

Dynamic Cycle Theory

Gann measured time in trading days from major pivots. When a fixed number of trading days from a past high/low aligns with the present, the market is "due" to react. Harmonic counts used:

  • 45 trading days (≈ 9 weeks)
  • 90 trading days (≈ 18 weeks / 1 quarter)
  • 120 trading days (≈ 6 months)
  • 180 trading days (≈ 1 year × ½)
  • 270 trading days (≈ 1 year × ¾)
  • 360 trading days (≈ 1 full market year)
